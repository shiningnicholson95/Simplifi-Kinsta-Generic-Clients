What Is a Date-of-Death Appraisal?

An IRS-Qualified Appraisal for Estate Tax Compliance

A date-of-death appraisal is a certified, retrospective property valuation establishing fair market value as of the exact day the owner passed away. Because the IRS and estate courts require value as of that specific historical point — not today's market — the appraiser must research comparable sales and market conditions from that period. Under Treasury Regulation §20.2031-1, this must be prepared by a "qualified appraiser" to be accepted for Form 706 estate tax purposes.

Whether you're a CPA preparing an IRS Form 706 filing, an executor establishing the stepped-up cost basis for inherited real property, or an estate attorney managing an alternate valuation date election, McKoy Harris Valuation delivers a thorough, IRS-compliant retrospective appraisal — backed by deep market knowledge across Chester, Delaware, Montgomery, and Philadelphia counties.

When You Need This

Common Situations Requiring a Date-of-Death Appraisal

IRS Form 706 Estate Tax ReturnEstates exceeding the federal exemption must report real property at fair market value. The IRS requires a qualified appraisal from a certified appraiser — a Zillow estimate or agent's CMA will not satisfy this requirement.

Stepped-Up Cost BasisHeirs receive a new cost basis equal to the date-of-death value. A certified appraisal establishes this figure and protects beneficiaries from overpaying capital gains tax on a future sale.

Alternate Valuation Date ElectionUnder IRC §2032, executors may elect to value the estate 6 months after death if total estate value declined. We can appraise as of either date — just tell us which your CPA requires.

Multi-Property EstatesWhen the estate includes multiple residential properties, vacation homes, or rental units, each requires its own certified date-of-death appraisal with a separate value conclusion.

IRS Audit ResponseIf the estate tax return is selected for examination, the appraiser's certified report and documentation become the primary defense. A well-supported, USPAP-compliant appraisal from a qualified appraiser is your strongest protection.

Portability Election (DSUE)Estates electing to transfer the deceased spouse's unused exemption to the surviving spouse require a defensible property value as the baseline for the portability calculation.
