What Is an Estate & Probate Appraisal?

A Certified Value That Holds Up with the IRS and PA Courts

An estate appraisal — also called a date-of-death appraisal or retrospective appraisal — establishes the fair market value of a property as of a specific past date, typically the day the owner passed away. Unlike a standard current-value appraisal, this requires the appraiser to research comparable sales and market conditions from that historical period, making certified credentials and methodology critical.

Whether you are an executor navigating the Pennsylvania probate process, an estate attorney preparing an inventory for the Register of Wills, a CPA calculating stepped-up cost basis, or an heir managing the distribution of a family home, McKoy Harris Valuation delivers a thorough, defensible report — backed by deep knowledge of Chester, Delaware, Montgomery, and Philadelphia County real estate markets.

When You Need This

Common Situations That Require an Estate Appraisal

Probate Court FilingPennsylvania probate requires an inventory of estate assets including real property. A certified appraisal is the recognized standard for establishing that value with the Register of Wills.

IRS Estate Tax Return (Form 706)Estates above the federal exemption threshold must report real property at fair market value on Form 706. The IRS expects a qualified appraisal prepared by a certified appraiser.

Stepped-Up Cost BasisHeirs who inherit real property receive a new cost basis equal to the date-of-death value. A certified appraisal establishes this figure and minimizes future capital gains tax exposure on a sale.

Equitable Distribution Among HeirsMultiple heirs sharing an estate need a neutral, certified value to divide real property assets fairly — whether through a buyout, sale, or trust distribution.

Sale of Inherited PropertyBefore listing an inherited home, executors often need a certified value to establish a fair asking price, confirm equity, and support the estate's tax basis calculations.

Trust AdministrationLiving trusts or testamentary trusts holding real property need certified valuations for trustee reporting, periodic accounting, and distributions to beneficiaries.

Your Estate Appraisal, Step by Step

Submit Your Request

Complete the online request form or call (610) 871-0318. Provide the property address, the date of death (your appraisal's effective date), and your role — executor, attorney, heir, or CPA. If you have a probate filing deadline, mention it so we can prioritize accordingly.

Document Review & Scheduling

Antwan will follow up within one business day to confirm the effective date, discuss any access challenges (vacant homes, multi-party coordination, estate locks), and schedule the property inspection. For occupied or tenant-occupied inherited homes, we coordinate access with all relevant parties.

Property Inspection

Antwan conducts a thorough interior and exterior inspection — photographing all rooms, noting current condition, confirming square footage, and documenting all features relevant to value. Estate properties in deferred maintenance or poor condition are assessed honestly and accurately. Typically takes 45–90 minutes.

Retrospective Market Research & Report Preparation

Using MLS data and public records, Antwan identifies comparable sales from the period surrounding the date of death, applies appropriate adjustments for the conditions of that market, and prepares a detailed USPAP-compliant retrospective appraisal. Standard reports are completed within 5–7 business days.

Report Delivery

Your completed appraisal report is delivered via secure PDF within 24 hours of completion — ready for filing with the Register of Wills, submission to the IRS, or distribution to all parties. Hard copies are available on request. Antwan is available to answer follow-up questions from attorneys or CPAs reviewing the report.
