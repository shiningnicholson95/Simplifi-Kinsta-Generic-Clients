What Is a Tax Appeal Appraisal?

Your Certified Evidence for a Lower Property Tax Bill

When a county assessor overvalues your property, you overpay taxes every single year until you successfully appeal. In Pennsylvania, homeowners have the right to challenge their assessment before the county Board of Assessment Appeals. A certified appraisal from a licensed PA appraiser is the most persuasive evidence in that hearing — far more credible than informal comparable sales printouts, an automated estimate, or a real estate agent's opinion.

McKoy Harris Valuation prepares certified, USPAP-compliant appraisals specifically for tax appeal purposes — documenting your home's true market value with the methodology and documentation that boards and courts expect. A successful appeal delivers savings that compound every year the lower assessment remains in effect.

When You Need This

Common Situations That Warrant a Tax Appeal

Recent Assessment IncreaseYou received a notice that your property's assessed value jumped significantly. A certified appraisal can demonstrate the true market value and support a reduction to the correct level.

Purchase Price Below Assessed ValueBuying a home for less than the county's assessed value is strong prima facie evidence of over-assessment. The purchase price documents the market's verdict on value.

Comparable Homes Assessed LowerNeighboring properties with similar features, size, and location have noticeably lower assessments — indicating inconsistent or inaccurate application of the county's assessment methodology.

Post-Market-DeclineA regional market downturn has pushed values below your assessed level. The county's stale assessment no longer reflects current market reality — and you're overpaying as a result.

Appeal Window OpenPA counties have annual appeal deadlines (August 1 in Chester County). The window opens once a year — if you've been overpaying, every year you don't appeal is money left on the table permanently.

Property in Poor ConditionYour home has deferred maintenance, structural issues, or functional obsolescence not reflected in the county's assessment — a certified appraisal documents the actual condition-adjusted value.

Common Questions About Tax Appeal Appraisals

In Chester County, the annual appeal filing deadline is typically August 1st for the following tax year. Delaware County and Montgomery County also have annual appeal windows, but deadlines vary by county — contact your county's Board of Assessment Appeals or visit their website to confirm. Missing the deadline means waiting until the next appeal cycle, so act early once you receive your assessment notice.

Savings depend on the gap between the assessed value and the certified market value, and your municipality's millage rate. In Chester County, a $50,000 reduction in assessed value can translate to $700–$1,200 in annual tax savings depending on your municipality and school district. Those savings compound every year the lower assessment remains in effect — making a successful appeal one of the highest-ROI moves a homeowner can make.

The Board is not required to adopt your appraisal's value verbatim — they weigh all evidence presented. However, a certified USPAP-compliant appraisal from a licensed appraiser is the most credible evidence you can present and is far more persuasive than informal comparable sales or an agent's opinion. A well-documented, methodology-transparent report significantly improves your chances of a meaningful reduction.

Yes. Pennsylvania homeowners can file a tax appeal pro se (without an attorney) before the county Board of Assessment Appeals. Many successful appeals are handled directly by homeowners armed with a certified appraisal as their evidence. If the Board denies your appeal or the reduction is insufficient, you can appeal their decision to the Pennsylvania Court of Common Pleas within 30 days of the Board's written decision.
