What Is a Foreclosure Appraisal?

The As-Is Certified Value That Drives Foreclosure Decisions

A foreclosure appraisal is a certified, USPAP-compliant opinion of a property's as-is fair market value — documenting the property's current condition, including any deferred maintenance, damage, or vacancy impact, without assuming repairs. Lenders, servicers, courts, and regulatory agencies require this standard because it reflects what an informed buyer would actually pay in the current market, not a hypothetical repaired condition.

McKoy Harris Valuation provides certified foreclosure appraisals for the full spectrum of distressed property situations — short sale approvals, loss mitigation reviews, REO pricing and listing, deficiency judgment proceedings, and pre-foreclosure equity assessments. Reports are delivered with lender-ready documentation: USPAP compliance certification, PA license, and E&O insurance.

When You Need This

Six Foreclosure Scenarios That Require a Certified As-Is Appraisal

Short Sale ApprovalLenders and servicers require a certified as-is appraisal before approving a short sale to confirm the proposed price reflects actual market value — protecting against below-market sales to connected buyers and satisfying investor requirements.

Loss Mitigation ReviewServicers evaluating loan modification, forbearance, repayment plan, and deed-in-lieu options require a current as-is valuation to assess the property's value relative to the outstanding loan balance and guide the appropriate workout option.

REO Listing & PricingAfter foreclosure is complete, lenders and servicers need a certified as-is appraisal to establish a market-based listing price, satisfy OCC/FDIC regulatory documentation requirements, and support investor reporting on the asset.

Deficiency JudgmentIn Pennsylvania, lenders may seek a deficiency judgment after sheriff's sale for the difference between the loan balance and the sale proceeds. A certified as-is appraisal establishing the property's market value at the time of sale is critical evidence in deficiency proceedings on both sides.

Pre-Foreclosure Equity AssessmentHomeowners facing default who want to understand their actual equity position before engaging with a servicer or deciding between sale, short sale, deed-in-lieu, or reinstatement — an independent appraisal is the most reliable starting point.

Regulatory Compliance (OCC/FDIC)Federally regulated lenders are required to obtain independent appraisals for real estate transactions above threshold amounts. REO dispositions and distressed asset sales involving regulated institutions require certified USPAP-compliant valuations that satisfy OCC and FDIC documentation standards.

Common Questions About Foreclosure Appraisals

A foreclosure appraisal is a certified, USPAP-compliant opinion of a property's as-is fair market value — reflecting the property's current condition without assuming repairs or improvements. Unlike a standard purchase appraisal, a foreclosure appraisal documents all deferred maintenance, damage, and deficiencies to establish the true current market value. This as-is standard is required for short sale approvals, loss mitigation decisions, REO pricing, and deficiency judgments.

A short sale occurs when a homeowner sells the property for less than the outstanding mortgage balance, with lender approval. Before approving a short sale, the lender or servicer must confirm the proposed sale price reflects actual as-is market value — preventing below-market sales and satisfying investor requirements. A certified USPAP appraisal is the primary evidence lenders use to evaluate short sale approval requests and is typically required by GSE and private investor guidelines.

REO (Real Estate Owned) refers to property that has reverted to the lender after a completed foreclosure. Lenders and servicers require a certified as-is appraisal before listing REO properties to establish a market-based price, satisfy OCC/FDIC regulatory documentation requirements, and support investor reporting on the asset.
